# Problem

HTTP 503 Service Unavailable means the responder cannot currently serve the request. It may be generated by Service B, a gateway with zero healthy upstreams, a circuit breaker, overload protection, maintenance policy, or rate/admission control.

Status 503 is not a root cause. Identify the generator and its product-specific subreason.

# Production Situation

At `2026-09-13T19:20:22Z`, all A calls through Envoy to Inventory return 503.

* requestId `ord-2f4d72`, traceId `0bf92f3577b34da6a3ce929d0e0e000b`
* A `order-a-4.18.2-k2m5q`, Envoy `gw-3`
* B rollout version `7.6`, instances in zones a/b/c
* gateway route `POST /v1/inventory/reservations`
* normal: 230/s, success 99.96%, p99 180 ms, healthy targets 3
* abnormal: 503 100% in 4-7 ms, flag `UH`, healthy targets 0
* health checks to 8081 refuse; direct B business calls to 8080 take 118-134 ms
* B CPU 27%, heap 52%, queue 0, DB pool has idle capacity

# Architecture

```text
Order A --healthy--> Envoy gateway
                         |
                         +-- health check :8081 X refused
                         |
                         `-- user upstream :8080 not attempted
                               Inventory B 7.6 is actually listening
```

Direct B success proves the application can serve on 8080, not that the gateway considers it eligible.

# What I Check FIRST

1. **503 generator/subreason.** WHAT: headers, gateway ID, response flag. WHY: 503 meanings differ. LOOK FOR: Envoy `UH` = no healthy upstream in this deployment.
2. **Latency shape.** WHAT: time to 503. WHY: 5 ms suggests local rejection; 5 s suggests timeout or queue. LOOK FOR: 4-7 ms.
3. **Healthy target count/reasons.** WHAT: eligible/unhealthy counts by target and reason. WHY: zero targets prevents attempts. LOOK FOR: `connection_refused:8081`.
4. **Direct versus gateway contract.** WHAT: target port/path/protocol/identity. WHY: direct may bypass eligibility. LOOK FOR: B serves 8080.
5. **Rollout/config change.** WHAT: management port and gateway health config. WHY: old targets drained after B 7.6. LOOK FOR: stale 8081.

# Step-by-Step Investigation

### Step 1 - Establish the responder

* **What I check:** `server`, gateway instance, request ID, status, flag/body error code.
* **Why:** B-generated overload 503 needs a different owner.
* **Expected result:** normal 201 forwarded from B.
* **Bad result:** Envoy 503 `UH`.
* **Meaning:** in this Envoy config no healthy upstream is available.
* **Next branch:** inspect target eligibility before B application.

### Step 2 - Validate A-to-gateway transport

* **What I check:** A DNS, TCP, TLS, gateway receive rate, downstream duration.
* **Why:** the gateway may be reachable while B path is unavailable.
* **Expected result:** all complete below 20 ms and counts match.
* **Bad result:** gateway emits 503 in 5 ms.
* **Meaning:** immediate policy/eligibility rejection, not B response latency.
* **Next branch:** compare healthy/unhealthy target gauges.

### Step 3 - Inspect target health reason

* **What I check:** target count, per-target state, check port/path/protocol, last result.
* **Why:** "unhealthy" is a conclusion of a configured probe.
* **Expected result:** three healthy targets.
* **Bad result:** zero healthy; all checks refused on 8081.
* **Meaning:** the probe cannot open its configured port.
* **Next branch:** compare health configuration to actual listeners.

### Step 4 - Compare listener and probe contract

* **What I check:** B `ss`, Service targetPort, management port, readiness, gateway health port.
* **Why:** Running/Ready does not guarantee the gateway's separate check is correct.
* **Expected result:** health handler listens on configured 8081.
* **Bad result:** only 8080 listens and direct business request succeeds.
* **Meaning:** targets are falsely excluded by stale health configuration.
* **Next branch:** inspect B 7.6 and gateway config diffs.

### Step 5 - Distinguish other 503 branches

* **What I check:** overflow/breaker flags, maintenance rules, B access logs, queue/rejections, rate-limit headers.
* **Why:** 503 can represent actual overload or policy.
* **Expected result:** no upstream attempt for `UH`.
* **Bad result:** B span exists with executor rejection, or Envoy flag shows overflow.
* **Meaning:** investigate B capacity or gateway breaker, not target health.
* **Next branch:** follow the named generator's queue/threshold.

### Step 6 - Analyze direct versus gateway path fairly

* **What I check:** same target, route, protocol, identity, safe payload, and time.
* **Why:** direct call bypasses gateway route and health selection.
* **Expected result:** gateway and direct both work.
* **Bad result:** direct 8080 returns 201 in 126 ms while gateway never attempts upstream.
* **Meaning:** intermediary eligibility/config differs.
* **Next branch:** correct probe; never force targets healthy without validation.

### Step 7 - Preserve and mitigate

* **What I check:** old/new port contract, target transition timing, peer capacity, retries.
* **Why:** old targets draining explains sudden zero after gradual rollout.
* **Expected result:** config changes atomically with service.
* **Bad result:** final old target drains while checks for all new targets refuse.
* **Meaning:** incompatible rollout sequencing.
* **Next branch:** restore correct check port or roll back, then observe targets re-enter one by one.

### 503 result branches by generator

| Result | What it proves | What it does not prove | Exact next check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Envoy `UH` | No healthy upstream was eligible in that Envoy view | Every B process is down | Inspect per-target health reason and discovery reconciliation |
| Envoy pending overflow | Gateway admission capacity was exceeded | B is overloaded | Inspect pending limits, connection pools, demand, and B capacity |
| Circuit-breaker rejection | A protective threshold opened | Dependency caused the original failures | Inspect breaker trigger window and downstream error/latency |
| B-generated 503 with rejection log | Request reached B and B rejected work | CPU is the limiting resource | Inspect executor active/max/queue, pool pending, and admission reason |
| Maintenance-policy 503 | A configured policy intentionally blocks traffic | B cannot serve | Confirm change window, scope, owner, and bypass controls |
| 503 with `Retry-After` | Responder gives a retry hint | Retrying is safe or within deadline | Check idempotency, remaining budget, jitter, and downstream capacity |
| Health refusal | Probe reached a closed port | User listener is also closed | Compare probe and traffic port/listener |
| Health timeout | Probe did not finish in its budget | Network is dropping traffic | Split connect versus response and inspect probe handler load |
| Health HTTP 404 | An HTTP responder lacks that path | Target process is unhealthy | Compare configured path/context root and expected status matcher |
| Direct 201; gateway `UH` | B serves direct equivalent while gateway excludes it | Gateway config is the root cause | Compare exact probe identity, protocol, inventory, and route |

### Eligibility timeline

At 19:14, B 7.6 canary starts and Kubernetes readiness passes on 8080.

At 19:15, Envoy probes 8081 and records `connection_refused`.

At 19:16, two old B 7.5 targets still carry user traffic, masking incompatibility.

At 19:18:32, the final old target drains and healthy target count becomes zero.

At 19:20, the page fires because every request gets fast 503 `UH`.

This timeline explains why the incident starts after the rollout began rather
than at the first new pod.

If healthy count had decreased one target at a time with increased peer queue,
I would evaluate actual capacity as well as probe compatibility.

### Readiness and health semantics

Kubernetes readiness and Envoy health are independent consumers of potentially
different endpoints.

`Running` proves container lifecycle state; `Ready` proves the configured
Kubernetes probe passed; neither proves Envoy's 8081 check passes.

Liveness should not depend on a remote database because dependency failure
could trigger a restart storm.

Readiness may reflect whether a pod should receive traffic, but a separate
business synthetic is still needed for DB/auth/data-path correctness.

Forcing targets healthy bypasses safety and can route users to an unknown
state; correct the probe contract instead.

### Incremental recovery checks

After correcting the port, I expect healthy count to change 0 -> 1 -> 2 -> 3.

For each admission I verify the target receives a bounded share and its p99,
error rate, queue, and DB pool match peers.

If health becomes green but upstream attempts remain zero, I inspect gateway
route/cluster propagation.

If attempts resume but B returns application 503, I switch to B's admission
reason rather than continuing the health-port branch.

If all technical checks recover but the reservation synthetic fails, I keep
the incident open and inspect the business dependency path.

# Metrics to Check

| Metric | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| 503 by generator/flag | High `UH` selects zero healthy upstream; B 503 requires B branch |
| Response duration | Low 4-7 ms means immediate gateway action; high can mean queue/work |
| Healthy target count | Low/zero explains no routing; high plus 503 suggests breaker/overflow/policy |
| Unhealthy reason | Refusal selects port/listener; timeout selects path/overload; 404 selects path |
| Gateway upstream attempts | Flat zero confirms no target selection; nonzero means later failure |
| Direct B success | High proves B route for that sample, not gateway eligibility |
| B server rate | Low/zero via gateway expected; per-source split avoids counting probes |
| Retry attempts | High repeats immediate 503 and adds gateway load without reaching B |
| B CPU/queue/pools | Low/flat supports false exclusion; high with B-generated 503 suggests overload |
| p50/p95/p99/max | All low during fast failures can look "faster"; combine with error/success |
| Per-version/target | New version only ties to contract change; verify exact diff |
| After correction | Healthy count should rise before user success; target balance then stabilizes |

# Root Cause

B 7.6 moved its management/health listener from 8081 to 8080, but Envoy health-check configuration remained on 8081.

```text
B rollout -> new targets listen 8080 only -> health checks refuse on 8081
-> gateway marks every target unhealthy -> no upstream attempt
-> Envoy emits 503 UH -> business unavailable
```

# Fix

**Immediate mitigation:** restore the correct health port through managed config or roll back B 7.6. Wait for each target to pass before restoring traffic.

**Root cause correction:** derive health port/path/protocol from the versioned service contract.

**Permanent fix:** rollout compatibility checks, minimum healthy-target gates, target reason alerts, and gateway-path business canaries. Do not force unhealthy state to healthy or add retries.

# Verification

Before: healthy targets 0, 503 230/s, upstream attempts 0, business success 0%.

After: healthy targets 3, unhealthy 0, 503 0/s, each target receives 75-78/s, gateway p99 179 ms, attempts/request 1.00, business success 99.97% for 30 minutes. Direct and gateway reservation synthetics both commit once.

# Prevention

* Alert on healthy-target count below safe capacity and each health reason.
* Block rollout when compatible targets would reach zero.
* Contract tests cover probe port/path/protocol and actual listener.
* Runbook starts with 503 generator/flag and distinguishes health, breaker, overload, maintenance, and B response.
* Dashboard overlays B rollout, target transitions, upstream attempts, and business success.
* Use shallow liveness, meaningful readiness, and a separate deep business synthetic.

# Interview Answer

### What I would say in an interview

I identify who generated 503 and inspect that product's reason. Envoy returned `UH` in 4-7 ms, and its healthy-target count had fallen from three to zero. Every health check was refused on 8081, while direct business calls to B 7.6 on 8080 succeeded. The B release moved the listener but gateway health config stayed stale. I restored the correct port, then versioned the probe contract and added zero-healthy-target rollout gates. I verified three eligible targets, zero 503s, balanced traffic, normal retries, and successful reservations through the gateway.

### Common interviewer traps

Do not equate 503 with B down, treat Kubernetes Ready as gateway healthy, or force targets healthy. A fast low-latency dashboard can represent total failure.

### Quick memory flow

503 -> generator -> subreason -> duration -> target eligibility -> probe/listener -> direct comparison -> restore -> balanced business verification.

# Interview Follow-up Questions

1. **Could a circuit breaker return 503?** Yes; identify breaker/overflow flags and trigger metrics.
2. **Why no B trace?** Gateway made no upstream attempt; still corroborate sampling/access logs.
3. **502 versus 503?** 502 often follows a failed/invalid upstream exchange; this 503 has no eligible upstream.
4. **503 versus 504?** This 503 is immediate; 504 normally follows waiting to an upstream deadline.
5. **Why direct success is insufficient?** It bypasses gateway target health, routing, and identity.
6. **Should readiness query the DB?** Not necessarily; avoid restart storms, and use separate deep synthetics.
7. **Why not add replicas?** The same wrong health check would reject them.
8. **How do you safely recover?** Admit corrected targets gradually and verify business success per target.
